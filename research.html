Research

Traffic engineering has long described behavior with parameters — a desired speed, a headway, a gap-acceptance threshold. Real drivers and real travelers do not think in parameters. Across the three threads below the question is the same: can behavior be learned from observation faithfully enough to stand in for the real thing? A full list of the work is on the Publications page.

Driving Behavior Modeling & Simulation

Understanding real-world driving behavior from vehicle trajectories and reproducing it through generative AI and microscopic traffic simulation. Trajectory data records what a vehicle did, not why: two drivers with the same speed profile may be doing entirely different things — one impatient in free flow, the other trapped behind a slow truck. I cluster observed trajectories into behavioral types, describe each type in a form a generative model can condition on, and turn those descriptions back into driving decisions. The generated agents are then placed in SUMO and VISSIM, where the question becomes empirical: does the synthetic population reproduce the distribution of the observed one?

Human Mobility Modeling & Synthesis

Modeling and generating individual-level mobility patterns from travel surveys and location data, with an emphasis on realism and privacy preservation. Individual mobility data is the most useful and the most sensitive dataset in transportation: travel surveys are rich but small, mobile location data is large but blunt, and neither can be released as collected. I generate synthetic individuals — full daily travel chains with plausible activity sequences, timing, and destinations — that hold at the aggregate level without exposing any real person. Conditioning the generator on survey evidence, rather than letting it invent, is what makes the output defensible.

Traffic Safety & Operations

Combining empirical data, AI, and traffic simulation to assess crash risk, predict hazardous interactions, and improve transportation system operations. Crash records are the ground truth for safety, but they are rare, lagging, and silent about the near-misses that precede them; microscopic simulation produces conflicts in abundance but has no claim to realism on its own. I build hybrid frameworks in which each corrects the other — crash data anchors the model where observations exist, and simulation extends risk estimates into conditions not yet observed. Related work covers hazardous driving indicators, child-pedestrian trajectory prediction, and LiDAR-based traffic measurement.
